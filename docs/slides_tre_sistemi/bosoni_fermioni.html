CHAPTER 7 · QUANTUM STATISTICS

Two identical particles, two ways to combine them

Place one particle in state a and the other in state b. Swapping the particle labels gives two descriptions of the same occupation. Add or subtract the quantum amplitudes to obtain a state with a definite sign under exchange.

ab⟷ exchange ⟷ba

Symmetric combination

|a,b⟩+|b,a⟩2

Exchange leaves the state unchanged. The combination is symmetric.

If both particles are in state a

|a,a⟩+|a,a⟩=2|a,a⟩

The contributions add. After normalization, the result is a state with two particles in the same quantum state. Particles that follow this rule are bosons.

Antisymmetric combination

|a,b⟩−|b,a⟩2

Exchange reverses the sign of the state. The combination is antisymmetric.

If both particles are in state a

|a,a⟩−|a,a⟩=0

The contributions cancel. Particles that follow this rule are fermions. Two identical fermions can only occupy distinct quantum states.

The first two formulas use distinct, orthogonal states. When a = b, the sum or difference is shown before normalization.
