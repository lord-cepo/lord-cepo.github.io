Sedimentation and hydrostatic equilibrium

Particles under gravity and the pressure profile

Orange shows pressure inferred from particle counts in horizontal bands, averaged over recent observations. Blue shows the equilibrium profile. Height z increases upwards. Random particle moves are accepted using the Metropolis rule at fixed temperature. Sweeps track sampling progress.

The weight of a fluid layer

Consider a thin layer of cross-sectional area A and thickness dz. Pressure at its base pushes upwards. Pressure on its upper face and the layer’s weight act downwards. At rest the forces balance.

p(z)A−p(z+dz)A=ρgAdz dpdz=−ρg

The greater the mass density ρ, the faster pressure falls with height. If ρ is constant, the decrease is linear.

Why this profile is exponential

The simulated column contains ideal particles of mass m. At fixed T, the ideal gas law gives p = ρkBT/m. Substituting this into the hydrostatic equation gives

p(z)=p(0)e−z/ℓ,ℓ=kBTmg

Counting particles by height gives the same profile. A higher position has greater potential energy mgz and a smaller Boltzmann weight. The length ℓ is the distance over which pressure falls by a factor of e.

Try g = 0 for uniform pressure, then increase gravity to concentrate particles near the bottom.
