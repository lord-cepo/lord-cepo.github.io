Temperature and chemical potential

Metropolis sampling uses insertions and removals, with optional swaps. Swaps conserve N. The chemical potential acts through moves that change N. The lattice is periodic in both directions, with no gravity.

A cluster is a connected set of 8 × 8 blocks whose density exceeds the threshold. After 200 burn-in sweeps, we count observations with a cluster spanning at least 16 blocks. Observations are correlated and the frequency depends on the threshold and resolution.

Try a temperature just above Tc with μ/ε = −2. Dense regions can form and dissolve. Lower μ slightly to make them rarer. Above Tc these are fluctuations without liquid–vapour coexistence.

The model is a 2D grand-canonical lattice gas. The Kelvin scale is calibrated by setting Tc = 647.096 K (IAPWS). Density, μ and clusters still refer to the lattice. This is a qualitative analogy for 3D water. The GPU updates one checkerboard colour at a time, so updated sites are never neighbours. Swaps randomly group same-colour sites into disjoint pairs so concurrent moves do not change one another’s ΔK.
