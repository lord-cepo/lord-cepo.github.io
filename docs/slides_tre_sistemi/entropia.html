Chapter 4 · From counting to entropy

Entropy makes state counting additive

Ω counts the microstates compatible with a macrostate. Its logarithm defines entropy.

1

The possibilities multiply

For two weakly coupled systems with fixed energies E₁ and E₂, every microstate of the first system can be paired with every microstate of the second.

Ω12(E1,E2)=Ω1(E1)Ω2(E2)

For example, 3 microstates and 4 microstates give 12 combinations.

2

The logarithm turns a product into a sum

S=kBlnΩS12=S1+S2

Entropy measures the number of possibilities on an additive scale. More accessible microstates mean higher entropy.

3

Temperature measures how the number of possibilities grows

β=(∂lnΩ∂E)N,V=1kB(∂S∂E)N,V=1kBT

The slope of S with respect to energy is 1/T. At thermal equilibrium the two systems have the same temperature, though their total energies can differ.

N and V are held fixed in the derivative. The product counts states for a fixed energy split. If only E₁ + E₂ is fixed, sum over all allowed splits.
