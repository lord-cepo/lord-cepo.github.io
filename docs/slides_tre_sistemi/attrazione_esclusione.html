Depletion attraction

Two disks immersed in a gas of smaller disks

All small disks have radius 0.50 R. Dashed outlines mark regions their centres cannot enter, and green shows the overlap. In this ideal-solvent model, small disks can overlap each other. They bounce elastically off the walls and the large disks, which move horizontally with a fixed midpoint.

Why moving closer frees up space

The large disks have radius R and the small ones have radius r = 0.50 R. A small disk’s centre must stay at least a = R + r from each large disk’s centre. When d < 2a, the two excluded regions overlap. Their union excludes less area because the overlap is counted only once, leaving more accessible area.
