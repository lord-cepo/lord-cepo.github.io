How many particles occupy a state?

Consider a single state of energy ε that can exchange particles with a reservoir at temperature T and chemical potential μ. With n particles its energy is nε. The Boltzmann weight, including the exchange with the reservoir, is qⁿ.

q=e−ε−μkBT,⟨n⟩=∑nnP(n)

Bosons

Allowed occupations are 0, 1, 2, …. To normalize, sum all the weights. For a bosonic state the sum converges when μ < ε, or equivalently q < 1.

Z=∑n=0∞qn=11−q,P(n)=(1−q)qn

To calculate the mean, weight each term by n. This sum follows by differentiating the geometric series and multiplying by q.

∑n=0∞nqn=qddq(11−q)=q(1−q)2 ⟨n⟩=q1−q=1eε−μkBT−1

For photons at equilibrium, μ = 0. This gives the mean occupation used in Planck’s law.

Fermions

The Pauli exclusion principle permits only the occupations 0 and 1. The sum of the weights has two terms.

Z=1+q,P(1)=q1+q

The empty state contributes zero to the mean, so the mean equals the probability of finding the state occupied.

⟨n⟩=0P(0)+1P(1)=P(1) ⟨n⟩=q1+q=1eε−μkBT+1

Here ε is the energy of one quantum state with a specified spin.
