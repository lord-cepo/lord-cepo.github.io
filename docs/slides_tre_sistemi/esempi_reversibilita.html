Two examples of symmetric transition probabilities

3 particles, 3 quanta. The destination is always chosen from all particles, including the source.

1. Choosing the source particle

X = (2, 1, 0)   ⇄   Y = (1, 1, 1)

RuleForward X → Y
particle 1 → 3		Reverse Y → X
particle 3 → 1
Source: random particle⅓ × ⅓ = 1/9⅓ × ⅓ = 1/9
Source: random packet⅔ × ⅓ = 2/9⅓ × ⅓ = 1/9

2. Including attempts from an empty particle

X = (3, 0, 0)   ⇄   Y = (2, 1, 0)

RuleForward X → Y
particle 1 → 2		Reverse Y → X
particle 2 → 1
Choose from all particles.
If the source is empty, count a step with no change.		⅓ × ⅓ = 1/9⅓ × ⅓ = 1/9
Try again until the source is occupied.1 × ⅓ = 1/3½ × ⅓ = 1/6

Equal forward and reverse probabilities ensure detailed balance for uniformly weighted occupation vectors. The alternative rules change these weights. This symmetry is sufficient to obtain the distribution, though other rules can also produce it.
