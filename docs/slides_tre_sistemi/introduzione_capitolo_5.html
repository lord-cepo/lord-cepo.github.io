From Boltzmann weights to free energy

At fixed temperature, a microstate of energy E has Boltzmann weight e−E/(kBT). If Ω configurations have the same energy, multiply by Ω to get their combined weight. The entropy of the group is S = kB ln Ω, quindi Ω = exp(S/kB), or exp(S) if entropy is measured in units of kB.

Ωe−E/(kBT)=eS/kBe−E/(kBT)=e−(E−TS)/(kBT)

The most probable group has the lowest value of E − TS. To compare equilibrium phases at fixed temperature, volume and particle number, use the Helmholtz free energy F = U − TS, where U is the mean internal energy.

E is energy and S is entropy. Arrows indicate higher (↑) or lower (↓) values relative to the other phase in each comparison.

Liquid and gas

In a liquid, particles stay close together and attraction lowers their energy. In a gas they can spread throughout much of the box without remaining in a compact cluster. This gives more accessible arrangements and greater entropy.

Solid and liquid

In a solid, particles oscillate around ordered positions. In a liquid, they stay close but can exchange places, and bonds between neighbours lose the common orientation of the crystal. These rearrangements increase the number of configurations.

As temperature rises, the term −TS becomes more important. At a phase transition, this entropic advantage can offset the energy required to evaporate or melt.
