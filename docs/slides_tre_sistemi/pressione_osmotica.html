The solute gains configurations

Consider Nₛ solute particles in a dilute ideal solution at temperature T. With the membrane in place they can occupy only Vₛ. Once it is removed, they can spread through the total volume V. Their configuration weight is proportional to the accessible volume raised to Nₛ.

ΔSmesc≈NskBlnVVs

In the dilute limit this expression gives the dominant contribution to mixing. For example, if the solute initially occupies half the vessel, the gain is NₛkB ln 2.

What pressure stops osmosis?

With the membrane in place, solvent entering the solution can increase its volume and give the solute more space. Osmotic pressure Π is the pressure difference between the solution and pure-solvent sides needed to stop the net flow. At equilibrium the work of expansion against this difference balances the entropy gain.

Π=T(∂S∂Vs)T,Ns≈NskBTVs=cRT

This is van ’t Hoff’s law. Here c is the molar concentration of the solute, nₛ/Vₛ. The pressure has the same dependence on concentration and temperature as an ideal gas.
