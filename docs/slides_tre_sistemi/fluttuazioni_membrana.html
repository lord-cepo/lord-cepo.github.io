CHAPTER 6 · SLIDE 7

Thermal fluctuations of a thin membrane

A surface undergoing thermal motion

The animation uses reduced units and a fixed vertical scale. The membrane has periodic boundaries and zero mean height. Stochastic relaxation samples the modes. The on-screen time is used to visualize equilibrium fluctuations.

How large are fluctuations at each wavelength?

Blue curve: equipartition. Orange points: sample averages grouped by the magnitude of q. When visible, the dashed line marks q× = √(σ/κ).

As in the bubble film, increasing the area costs energy. A small height fluctuation h adds area in proportion to |∇h|². Bending adds a curvature cost proportional to (∇²h)².

ℱ[h]=12∫[σ|∇h|2+κ(∇2h)2]dA

A Fourier mode of wave number q has stiffness σq² + κq⁴. Equipartition assigns the same mean energy to each quadratic degree of freedom. With the normalization used in the plot this gives

S(q)=kBTσq2+κq4

At fixed tension and rigidity, doubling T doubles fluctuation power. Tension dominates at small q, with S ∝ q⁻². Bending dominates at large q, with S ∝ q⁻⁴. Set σ to zero to isolate the latter case. Spectrum of fluctuating membranes
