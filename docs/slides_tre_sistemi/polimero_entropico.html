Entropic elasticity of a 2D polymer

A chain with fixed endpoints

Blue shows the exact count. Dashed orange shows the small-extension approximation. The chain has 40 segments of length a. Segments can cross and sites can be revisited. Playback samples ten paths per second. Each draw is independent and can repeat a path.

Counting conformations

Consider N steps of length a on a square lattice. A step can go right, left, up or down, with the same energy in every direction. Fix the first endpoint at (0, 0) and the second at (x, 0).

In coordinates u = (X + Y)/a and v = (X − Y)/a, each step corresponds to choosing two independent increments of ±1. Both sums must equal x/a, so each sequence needs (N + x/a)/2 positive increments.

Ω(x)=(N(N+x/a)/2)2,S(x)=kBlnΩ(x)

As the chain stretches, fewer paths reach the fixed endpoint. At full extension only one path is possible, with every step going right.

From entropy to Hooke’s law

For large N and x much smaller than the total length Na, applying Stirling’s approximation to the logarithm of the binomial coefficients gives a quadratic decrease in entropy.

S(x)−S(0)≈−kBx2Na2 F=TdSdx≈−2kBTNa2x

The minus sign means the chain pulls the movable end towards the fixed end. In this ideal model the stiffness grows with T because all conformations have the same internal energy.
