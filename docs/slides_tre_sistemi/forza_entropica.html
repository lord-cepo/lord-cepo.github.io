CHAPTER 6 · ENTROPIC FORCES

The entropic origin of pressure

A gas in a cylinder with a movable piston

ΔS/(NkB) = 0,000P/P₀ = 1,000

N and T are fixed, and the cross-sectional area A is constant. The dots show one particle configuration. The arrow shows the force the gas exerts on the piston.

Doubling x gives each particle twice as much space. The number of positional configurations grows by a factor of 2N, while the pressure is halved.

More volume, more configurations

The piston is at distance x from the wall, giving a volume V = Ax. In an ideal gas each particle can occupy the entire volume independently of the others. At fixed N and T, the position-dependent part of the statistical weight is proportional to VN. Counting configurations relative to the reference volume V₀ = Ax₀ gives

S(x)−S(x0)=NkBlnxx0.

From free energy to force

During a reversible displacement at constant temperature, the gas does work F dx on the piston. Its Helmholtz free energy, ℱ = U − TS, decreases by that amount. For an ideal gas at fixed T, internal energy U is independent of x, so differentiation at fixed T and N leaves the entropic contribution.

F=−(∂ℱ∂x)T,N=T(∂S∂x)T,N=NkBTx.

The ideal gas law

Divide the force by the piston area. Using V = Ax gives

P=FA=NkBTV,PV=NkBT=nRT

N is the number of particles and n is the number of moles. In general the force can also have an energetic contribution, −(∂U/∂x)T,N.
