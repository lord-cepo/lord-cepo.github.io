Grey marks weak local order and blue marks strong local order. Copies at the edges show the periodic boundaries.

The temperature sets the thermal bath. Changing density rebuilds the lattice. N = 196, with fixed area during each run, periodic boundaries in both directions and no gravity.

Gradually heat the crystal and watch the distant peaks in g(r) and the hexagonal alignment fade. Then cool it. Crystallization can take many sweeps and leave domains with different orientations.

Ψ₆ measures global coherence. The mean local magnitude measures order in individual neighbourhoods even when domains are misaligned. A hexatic phase can exist in 2D, but these two measures on a small system cannot identify it on their own.

V(r)=4ε[(σ/r)12−(σ/r)6]U=∑i<jVc(rij),Pacc=min(1,e−ΔU/(kBT))

Truncated and shifted potential: Vc(r) = V(r) − V(2.5σ) for r < 2.5σ and zero beyond. Each proposal displaces a particle by a vector drawn uniformly from [−0.12σ, 0.12σ]². One sweep is N proposals.

The calculation uses the six nearest neighbours and angles relative to the x axis. Ψ₆ = 1 for a perfect triangular crystal of any orientation. The mean local magnitude remains nonzero even in the liquid.

g(r)=A⟨Cr⟩N(N−1)ΔAr

Cr counts ordered pairs in an annulus of area ΔAr = π[(r + Δr)² − r²]. The 2D normalization includes a finite-N correction, so a uniform ideal gas has g(r) ≈ 1.
