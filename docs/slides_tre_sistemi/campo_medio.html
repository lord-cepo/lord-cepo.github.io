From microstates to the most probable density

Let M be the number of sites and N the number occupied. Each occupied pair contributes −4ε/M. There are N(N−1)/2 pairs, and at fixed N all configurations have the same energy.

E(N)−μN=−2εMN(N−1)−μN

Each microstate is weighted by its Boltzmann factor. To obtain the weight of N, multiply by the number of possible configurations.

P(N)∝(MN)e−(E−μN)/(kBT)

Con d = N/M, T* = kBT/ε and C = μ/ε, Stirling’s approximation gives log P(N)/M = f(d) plus a constant independent of d, up to corrections that vanish as M grows.

f(d)=2d2+CdT*−dlnd−(1−d)ln(1−d)

Red points mark global maxima. On the line C = −2 and for T* < 1, both maxima have equal exponential weight. They merge at the mean-field critical point T* = 1 and d = 1/2. The simulation with four neighbours instead has a critical temperature of T* ≈ 0.5673.
