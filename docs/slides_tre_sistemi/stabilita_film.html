Stability of a soap film without gravity

Two interfaces with an initial dip

Interface colour shows relative surfactant concentration, with darker areas indicating higher concentrations. Each change starts from the same profile. Heights are exaggerated and time is measured in model units.

Minimum thickness over time

Blue: film with the selected parameters. Dashed orange: the same film with E = G = 0 and the same mean tension K.

The film is uniform along y and periodic along x. Its thickness is h(x) = 1 + u(x), with interfaces at z = ±h/2. The midplane is fixed. The film starts with a small thickness perturbation and the model conserves the amount of liquid.

Tension K penalizes slopes because they increase the interfacial area. B is the local curvature of the interaction potential between the two surfaces. Negative B can favour thinning. Without additives, mode q grows if Kq² + B < 0.

Variable c measures the local excess of surfactant on the two surfaces. When E > 0, local tension falls where surfactant accumulates. Marangoni coupling draws liquid towards regions of higher tension. The term G(u − v) represents the transient elastic stress of the polymers. Variable v follows u on the timescale τ, allowing the remaining stress to relax.
