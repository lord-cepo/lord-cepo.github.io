Acoustic and optical modes in an atomic chain

How do the atoms move?

Blue: A atoms. Orange: B atoms, which are twice as massive. Grey dots mark equilibrium positions. The curves above the chain show the amplified longitudinal displacements of the two sublattices.

Frequency as a function of q

The cell of length a contains two atoms. Identical springs of stiffness K connect neighbours. The q axis runs from uniform motion to the edge of the first Brillouin zone.

Acoustic · neighbouring atoms move together

At q = 0 all atoms have the same displacement. The entire chain translates, the springs retain their length, and the frequency is zero. At small q, forces from neighbouring atoms nearly cancel. The restoring force is proportional to −q²u, so ω ≃ vs|q|.

Optical · atoms in a cell move in opposite directions

At q = 0 all cells repeat the same internal motion. A and B oscillate in opposite directions, stretching and compressing the springs. The restoring force and frequency remain finite. Since B is twice as massive, its displacement amplitude at q = 0 is half that of A.

ωott(0)=2K(1mA+1mB)

The figure shows longitudinal modes in a 1D chain. A 3D solid also has transverse polarizations.
